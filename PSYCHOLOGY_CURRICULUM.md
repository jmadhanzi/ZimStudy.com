# University & College Psychology Curriculum

This document outlines the comprehensive University and College Psychology curriculum added to the ZimStudy.com platform. This curriculum is designed to provide a complete education in psychology, from introductory courses to specialized and advanced topics.

## 1. Psychology Curriculum (17 Courses, 85 Topics)

The psychology curriculum covers a wide range of psychological disciplines, providing students with a deep understanding of human behavior, mental processes, and psychological science.

### Introductory & Foundational Psychology (3 courses)

- **Introduction to Psychology:** Comprehensive overview of psychology covering major theories, research methods, biological bases of behavior, sensation and perception, learning, memory, cognition, motivation, emotion, personality, social psychology, and mental health.
- **Educational Psychology:** Application of psychological principles to teaching and learning, including cognitive development, learning theories, motivation, classroom management, assessment, and individual differences in educational settings.
- **Psychology: High School Course:** Introduction to psychology designed for high school students, covering fundamental concepts in behavior, mental processes, and psychological science in an accessible format.

### Developmental Psychology (3 courses)

- **Human Growth & Development:** Study of physical, cognitive, and socioemotional development across the lifespan from conception through death, including major developmental theories and research.
- **Life Span Developmental Psychology:** Comprehensive examination of human development from conception through death, emphasizing physical, cognitive, and psychosocial changes across all life stages.
- **Psychology of Adulthood and Aging:** Focused study of psychological, biological, and social changes during adulthood and later life, including aging processes, cognitive changes, and successful aging.

### Social Psychology (2 courses)

- **Social Psychology:** Study of how individuals think about, influence, and relate to others, including topics such as social cognition, attitudes, conformity, group behavior, prejudice, and aggression.
- **Advanced Social Psychology:** In-depth examination of social psychological theories and research, including advanced topics in social cognition, interpersonal relationships, and applied social psychology.

### Abnormal Psychology (2 courses)

- **Abnormal Psychology:** Study of psychological disorders, including their symptoms, causes, and treatments. Covers anxiety, mood, personality, psychotic, and other mental health disorders.
- **Advanced Abnormal Psychology:** Advanced study of psychopathology, including detailed examination of diagnostic criteria, etiology, and evidence-based treatments for psychological disorders.

### Cognitive Psychology (1 course)

- **Cognitive Psychology:** Study of mental processes including perception, attention, memory, language, problem-solving, decision-making, and reasoning.

### Research Methods (1 course)

- **Research Methods in Psychology:** Introduction to research design, data collection, statistical analysis, and ethical considerations in psychological research. Includes experimental, correlational, and qualitative methods.

### Specialized Psychology (5 courses)

- **Industrial/Organizational Psychology:** Application of psychological principles to workplace settings, including employee selection, training, motivation, leadership, organizational culture, and work-life balance.
- **Psychology of Personality:** Study of personality theories, assessment methods, and research on individual differences in behavior, cognition, and emotion.
- **Physiological Psychology:** Study of the biological bases of behavior, including brain structure and function, neurotransmitters, hormones, and the relationship between biology and psychology.
- **History and Systems of Psychology:** Examination of the historical development of psychology as a science, including major schools of thought, influential figures, and contemporary perspectives.
- **Psychology of Motivation:** Study of factors that energize, direct, and sustain behavior, including biological drives, psychological needs, goals, and intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.

## 2. Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of these courses, students will be able to:

- Understand the major concepts, theories, and research findings in psychology.
- Apply psychological principles to personal, social, and organizational issues.
- Think critically about psychological research and media claims.
- Understand the biological, cognitive, and social bases of behavior.
- Pursue advanced studies in psychology and related fields.

This curriculum provides a strong foundation for careers in counseling, social work, education, business, research, and many other industries.
